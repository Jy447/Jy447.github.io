Kunlun well-log notes, reservoir parameters whose labels are an interpreter's formulas, law snapping, and where the points are lost

发表于 2026-10-25

A working log from the well-log problem of the PetroChina algorithm challenge (中国石油算法挑战赛, preliminary round on the Kunlun platform), written on 2026-10-25 while the round is still open. It closes on 2026-10-30, and advancement uses a hidden board scored after the close. The post describes the task, what the labels turned out to be, the model, the measurements and the dead ends, not the package code. All numbers are as of the writing date.

The round has four problems: pump anomaly warning, fracturing oil-gain prediction, well-log reservoir parameters and reservoir connectivity. On the pump and fracturing boards the tenth place sits at 100.0 and 98.02, scores no forecast produces, so those two got the remaining effort and the well-log board, the one with an ordinary spread (first place 84.38, tenth about 84.1, fiftieth 83.53), got most of it. Our package stands at 83.479, rank 62, up from the team’s 82.16 baseline of 09-14.

1. The task

  • 80 training wells with 730,268 depth points and 10 test wells with 95,948 points, at a 0.1 m step, one text file per well.
  • Inputs per depth point: depth, gamma ray (GR), photoelectric factor (PE), spontaneous potential (SP), caliper (CAL), sonic travel time (AC), bulk density (DEN), neutron (CNL), two resistivities (RXO shallow, RT deep), deviation and azimuth, bit size, casing.
  • Targets: porosity POR in percent, permeability PERM in mD, water saturation SW in percent. Non-reservoir rows carry the defaults 0.1 / 0.01 / 99.9, each target with its own default mask, so each target is a gate decision (default or continuous) and a value decision. Continuous rows are 32 % (POR), 31 % (PERM) and 28 % (SW) of the training rows.
  • The metric is tolerance-based and discontinuous:

A default row scores a full point only when the prediction is the default too. Outside the tolerance, extra regression accuracy earns nothing.

  • The public A-board scores 5 of the 10 test wells, the hidden B-board all 10. Per-well out-of-fold scores have a standard deviation of 4.6, so the A-board carries about 2 points of standard error. Five submissions per problem per China date, and a package must reproduce its result from the submitted code or the team is disqualified.

2. What the labels turned out to be

The labels are not a smooth function of the logs. They are an interpreter’s discrete formulas, chosen per sub-layer.

Target Structure Numbers
POR 11 linear laws of one curve, chosen per sub-layer: 5 density laws POR = b + s · DEN and 6 sonic laws POR = b + s · AC for example POR = 136.5 − 50.0 DEN, POR = 118.449 − 43.7532 DEN, POR = 0.2253 AC − 37.91; 53 % of continuous rows fit exactly one law within 0.25 porosity units, 29 % fit two or more, 18 % none
PERM 7 laws log10 PERM = a + s · POR, chosen per row for example log10 PERM = 0.1355 POR − 2.183; the same-law stretch is one row at the median, so the law choice is noisy
SW Archie with n = 2 and m = 2 (some m = 1.86): log10 SW = c − 0.5 log10 RT − log10 POR, using the label POR and the raw RT the constant c (the water resistivity) changes about 62 times per well in an erratic sequence; no statistic of the logs computes it

Other measurements on the labels:

  • 5,089 reservoir layers (runs of continuous POR), median 26 rows. The porosity family (density or sonic) is constant inside a layer (purity 0.99) and switches between consecutive layers 12 % of the time; sonic-law layers are the shallow, high-porosity, low-density, low-GR ones.
  • Inside a layer the law switches in contiguous depth sub-intervals, but the switch points sit on no curve inflection and the laws overlap in curve range, so no piecewise function of DEN or AC reproduces a layer.
  • The gate is a reservoir flag drawn per zone, not a cutoff on porosity: continuous POR labels go down to 0, and the would-be law porosity of default rows beside a zone has the same distribution as the zone’s edge rows. Gate misses are whole thin layers (median 15 rows, 66 % below 10 porosity units, high resistivity), not edges.
  • The SW labels use the raw RT: inside a continuous run the Archie constant computed with the raw RT is flat (median deviation 0.004 in log10), and every smoothed RT or RXO fits worse.
  • SW constant jumps and POR-law switches share boundaries part of the time: the probability of a POR-law switch is 0.40 at an SW jump against 0.057 where SW is calm. The SW constant does not transfer between wells by absolute depth.
  • Whole-well fits looked poor only because the constants change by zone: within 1 to 10 m windows POR is linear in the logs with R² 0.991 to 0.998, and SW is Archie with slope −0.495 on log RT.

3. The model, piece by piece

The package is one directory with the same five scripts for each well-fold split: train, calibrate, predict, validate, make the code zip. Two splits of the 80 wells into 5 folds are trained and averaged (10 fold directories).

Piece What it is Why

The SW models add 61 features from a nested cross-fitted porosity (486 in total) the Rwa quantile features lifted SW regression on continuous rows from 0.174 to 0.180 and are the top feature in every fold; exact porosity inside the SW features matters (SW 0.194 with the predicted porosity, 0.230 with the label porosity)

7. What did not work

  • Formula targets for SW: predicting the zone constant with the trees and rebuilding SW (0.151 against 0.174 direct); the constant’s spread across zones (0.227 log units) exceeds SW’s own (0.103). Snapping SW to a law: 21 to 22 % law accuracy, worse even with the label porosity. Even with the true water/oil labels and the label porosity, the law that best separates water from oil rows within ±15 m identifies the zone’s constant on only 44 % of rows.
  • SW sequence models on the 10-25 pass: Archie-constant smoothing in SW space, latent-law Viterbi decoding, quantile residuals, censored constant regression, direct optimisation of the official SW score, a 10-fold data-size probe (+0.0013). All negative or inside noise.
  • Law choice variants: one law per layer (−0.009), order-constrained Viterbi over depth (+0.002 as smoothing, worse when enforcing a law order), consensus votes per layer, analog-well transfer by depth shift (28 % right against 45 % for the classifier), a bigger law classifier (+0.0026 at twice the cost), a 13-class model replacing gate plus router (a tie), a GPU dilated-convolution + BiGRU law network (0.42 against 0.47 for LightGBM).
  • Gates: per-row expected-score gate (−0.03), hysteresis and length rules (+0.01), boundary-position features (0), predictable per-well thresholds.
  • Regressors and losses: L2 and linear-leaf trees (worse than L1), hedging SW and PERM between the default and the regression (−0.04), a mode-seeking Welsch refinement of the L1 member (+0.02, not worth shipping alone), a multi-task MLP on the features (worse alone, +0.02 blended), a dilated CNN on the engineered features (78.67 alone, +0.05 blended).
  • Every neural plug-in from the paper notes: SE, SK, FiLM, MoE, ALiBi, external memory, HiPPO, query pooling, DyT, engram, chunking, gradient reversal, retrieval priors, quantisation. None beats the tree router (0.33 against 0.39 routing accuracy for the best of them).
  • Depth-context stackers (LightGBM and neural) on top of the out-of-fold predictions.
  • More data in one model: an all-80-wells model set at weight 0.5 (−0.07 on the board; it cannot be calibrated), a third well-fold split (−0.008 OOF), ten folds instead of five (+0.0013).
  • Global knobs on the board (10-25): five probes, every one between −0.075 and +0.0026. Individually favourable thresholds do not compose: applying POR 0.65 and SW 0.95 together to the stronger package lost 0.075. The response surface is non-additive, and knob stacking is not a route to +0.6.

8. Lessons

  1. Read the label generator before modelling. Whole-well regressions looked like a noisy physical relation; 1 to 10 m windows showed exact linear laws, and a greedy search for sharp peaks in the label residuals over slope grids recovered them. That one finding is worth more than everything else combined.
  2. A tolerance metric rewards exact identification, not accuracy. Once a prediction is outside 8 % or 5 %, it earns zero, so a router that lands on the right law beats a regressor that is 6 % off everywhere.
  3. When the label is ambiguous (29 % of rows fit several laws), the definition of the router’s target is a lever. Five definitions of “the right law” are complementary and add up; more seeds of one definition saturate at three.
  4. The A-board is five wells with about 2 points of standard error, and a 0.1 OOF gain transfers at a fraction. Small levers need a cross-fitted OOF over all 80 wells before a slot is spent.
  5. Reproduce before submitting. The rules disqualify code that does not reproduce the result, and the fracturing problem showed that unpinned library versions change tree models by 1 to 10 % per well. Every well-log package passes a clean-room rerun with an identical SHA256.
  6. Machine notes that cost hours: a stopped cloud pod can resume on another host with an empty container disk, so weights and zips go to the network volume; three concurrent CatBoost GPU jobs stall a 16 GB card; kill processes only by PID or exact script name.

9. What comes next

  1. A joint latent-state decoder: the POR law, the PERM law and the SW constant decoded together, using the shared boundaries measured in section 2 (a POR-law switch is seven times more likely at an SW jump). Continue only if it reaches +0.20 OOF or +0.05 on continuous-row POR.
  2. SW state reconstruction with a different observation model: infer the water/oil boundary from local evidence, solve the Archie constant from the water side, allow abrupt changes, keep the direct regression as a low-confidence fallback. This is the only model-side direction with a credible route to more than half a point.
  3. A test-prior router as a low-weight sixth group (+0.0058 total OOF, measured), worth one package probe but not a new pipeline.
  4. Not planned: larger sequence models, more fold splits, more individual POR laws, generic features already among the 425, and per-row smoothing over the same probabilities. All of these have produced gains below +0.05 OOF and transferred at a fraction of that.

OSINT Method

发表于 2026-10-25

Goal

Use public data to uncover deeper information about missing persons (MPs) in these categories: FRIENDS, FAMILY, EMPLOYMENT_EDUCATION, BASIC_SUBJECT_INFO, ROMANTIC_PARTNERS, ADVANCED_SUBJECT_INFO, DAY_LAST_SEEN, ADVANCING_THE_TIMELINE (ATT, a flag that is rarely submitted) and LOCATION. Even in a world as connected as ours, many people are still missing.

Some skills

For simplicity, I call the missing person “he”, whether the person is male or female.

Social media matters most. Relatives and friends post appeals asking for information and tracing the person’s movements. On some forums, people who were near the places involved report that they may have seen the MP.

A Google account can reveal location history. If the MP’s phone is missing, the police can sometimes get records from the mobile carrier. That is hard for a locked iPhone, or for an eSIM registered in another country, for example when a tourist goes missing abroad. In a kidnapping or another extreme situation, someone else may control the MP’s phone and accounts, and may log in to post or delete things. Facebook offers a profile lock in some regions, but a locked profile may still be visible from other regions, so a VPN may help.

Some MPs have long criminal records and many different names. Some name changes are deliberate, for example to deceive the women they live with or to evade responsibility for what they did. Others come from family changes or other turning points in life, such as a marriage.

A court record may list only an assault, a larceny, property destruction, a false report, a failure to appear and one public intoxication, while the real underlying problem may be drug abuse.

The places where people live, work or travel range from good to bad. Whatever a person does leaves a trace, and even public data can bring deeper or hidden facts to light. For example, a hostel may say it has no luggage storage, yet the MP still left his luggage there. That suggests he knew the place well and had stayed there several times. He meant to come back for the luggage, but something stopped him.

The truth is hidden

Some MPs choose to disappear, to hide from or escape something. Others clearly vanish against their will.

Someone who disappears voluntarily may still stay in touch with the people closest to him. So if his Facebook friends A and B share a mutual friend C, C may be his new identity. People do not change overnight; there is usually a trigger. And even when people change, traces of their past remain. It is naive to assume that someone with a long criminal record will never offend again, although that does happen.

Involuntary disappearances have so many possible explanations that reaching an answer takes a great deal of effort and some luck.

Turn the idea around

This is the most important skill when you play Phoenix Wright in Ace Attorney. In complicated or unsolved cases, something that looks impossible may still have happened another way.

For example, a missing person had a ticket from A to B that was never scanned, and no camera at A saw him leave. Common sense says he is still somewhere around A and never reached B. But other solid clues show that he did reach B, by whatever means. And if that route is on the FBI’s list of high-intensity areas for commercial sex trafficking, the whole picture changes.

That is an extreme case. But look at it the other way: if everything that happened could be predicted and known, there would be no unsolved cases in the world.

Public data

The police can use non-public data. But whether the data is public or not, logic and reasoning are what matter most.

Alfa Connectome notes, a streaming GRU for order-book prediction, its ceiling, and what the data turned out to be

发表于 2026-10-24 更新于 2026-10-25

A working log from Wunder Fund’s Alfa Connectome challenge, written on 2026-10-24 while the competition is still running (deadline 2026-11-15, private re-scoring until 12-01). Section 7, added on 2026-10-25, condenses the full model log. The organisers ask participants not to publish complete solutions before the end, so this post describes the approach, the measurements and the dead ends, not the submission code. All numbers are as of the writing date.

1. The task

Each sequence is 20,000 rows of a limit order book stream with 112 anonymised features per row: for two instruments, i0 and i1, eleven bid and eleven ask price-like columns, their volumes, four trade-price and four trade-volume columns, plus eight extra columns. The targets t0 and t1 are two indicators of the future price movement of i0. The metric is a global weighted Pearson correlation, weights |clip(y, ±2)|, computed only on rows flagged need_prediction and is_scored, per target and then averaged.

Inference runs on one CPU core with 16 GB of memory and 60 minutes for 1,970 test sequences, which is 39.4 million rows, about 90 microseconds per row. The training set has 10,607 sequences with labels but no scoring mask; the validation set has 1,873 sequences with labels and the mask. Five submissions per day.

2. What the models look like

The submission averages three members with fixed weights 0.5 / 0.25 / 0.25, 927,878 parameters in total, about 46 microseconds per row.

The GRU member (679,426 parameters)

The fusion member (124,226 parameters each, two seeds)

The look-ahead teacher (training only, never submitted)

There is no MLP stack, no attention and no normalisation layer inside the members. KAN and MLP heads, DyT residual GRU stacks, state attention, a delta memory, a Transformer student and an iTransformer teacher were all tried and scored within noise of this plain design or below it.

3. How they are trained

  1. Teachers on the training set (10,607 sequences, 3 epochs each): six small causal models (GRU 1x128, GRU 2x128, LSTM 1x128, two feature-branch GRUs, a distilled GRU; validation subset 0.689 to 0.696) and the look-ahead teachers that read the next 25 or 50 rows.
  2. Students on the training set: 3 epochs, cosine schedule, peak learning rate 2e-3, 64 sequences per batch, truncated back-propagation in 1,024-row chunks. The loss is only the weighted distance to the teachers’ outputs (weights |clip(y)| plus a floor of 0.2), half on the six causal teachers and half on the look-ahead teacher. The GRU member distils the 25-row teacher, the fusion members the 50-row teacher.
  3. Cross-fitted soft labels on the validation set: the look-ahead teacher is fine-tuned five times, each with one fold of the 1,873 sequences held out, and labels that fold. No sequence is labelled by a teacher that saw its targets.
  4. Scored-row fine-tune: all parameters, 6 epochs, 32 sequences per batch, learning rate 2e-4 (1e-4 under test), weight decay 1e-4, gradient clip 1.0. Scored rows weigh 1, unscored rows 0.1, and the target is 0.2 times the true value plus 0.8 times the soft label. Two seeds per member, averaged in weight space.
  5. Package: the three averaged models combined 0.25 / 0.25 / 0.50 and exported to numba kernels with half-precision matrices, one compiled call per row.

Where the public score came from: a GRU 2x128 trained on the true labels scores 0.6479; the scored-row fine-tune adds +0.0071 (0.6550); the look-ahead teacher labels, the fusion members and the 2x256 size bring it to 0.6640; weight soups and a replay fine-tune add +0.0005 to 0.664539 (rank 66 of 5,334 at the time of writing). Of the +0.0167 total, about +0.0125 came from the labels the models are trained on and +0.0040 from the fine-tune procedure, soups and inference engineering. Architecture changes contributed nothing measurable.

One methodological lesson: a single-fold screen produced a false +0.0016 three times (mirror augmentation, a knob variant, output in ticks), and the 5-fold cross-fitted estimate wiped each of them out. Nothing goes to the leaderboard without the 5-fold gate (reference 0.663925).

5. What the data turned out to be

  • Four tick units (0.1254, 0.2413, 0.4685, 0.6569) that never change inside a sequence. The large-tick unit carries 47.7% of the metric weight and is predicted at a correlation of 0.44, against 0.70 to 0.72 for the other three. A perfect large-tick unit alone would add +0.18 to the score.
  • t0 = F minus the ask and t1 = the bid minus F, with one reference price F on the tick grid: F sits on a quote when the spread is 1 tick and on the midpoint when it is 2 ticks. F moves only when i0’s quotes move and is settled within roughly the next 8 to 16 quote changes, a few dozen to a few hundred rows.
  • t0 + t1 equals minus the current spread (98% of decoded quote changes agree). Knowing it would be worth +0.06, but it cannot be read from the features at the scored rows: the price columns are a clipped linear mix of the levels, and every reader sits at the base rate there.
  • The eight extra columns are clocks (time since the last quote change, the time step of the row, a slow clock); the trade columns are trade prints relative to the quotes. There is no instrument id, and clusters of sequences carry no exploitable information.
  • The scoring mask is not a rule on the local path (AUC 0.88 with future rows, 0.94 with the whole history), so it cannot be reproduced on the training set. The 1,873 validation sequences are the only fine-tuning data, and halving them costs 0.0018.
  • The training and validation sets come from different market regimes (separable with AUC 0.993). Training-regime sequences do not transfer to the test set even when selected and relabelled.

6. What comes next

  1. The 5-fold gate of the lower fine-tune learning rate. If it confirms at least +0.0005 on the package, all three members get full-data two-seed fine-tunes at the new rate, soups, a package and an upload; expected +0.001 to +0.002 public.
  2. The de-anonymised inputs. If fold 0 shows at least +0.001 for the GRU alone, they go through the 5-fold gate, then the decoder goes into the inference kernels (it runs at 10 microseconds per quote change, within budget), then full seeds.
  3. The two levers change different things, the optimiser and the inputs, so they should stack with each other and with the existing soups.
  4. Final selection: the window opens on 2026-11-01 at 23:59 UTC; the pool will be chosen by the 5-fold gate with the public score as a tie-break.

It also holds the exact training commands and run names needed to rebuild the package, which is why it stays private until the competition ends. What follows is the part worth keeping.

Two recipes run under these scores. The smoke recipe (a one-layer GRU of width 128 on 2,500 sequences, 1 to 3 minutes) screens dozens of ideas; the full recipe (all 10,607 sequences, seven teachers, then the scored-row fine-tune, about 25 minutes) draws conclusions. Of five positive smoke signals re-checked in the full recipe, one survived. The smoke control lacks a second nonlinear stage, so anything that adds one looks good there.

7.4 The package taken apart

Seventeen blocks were changed one at a time. The ones where the evidence says the choice matters:

  • Loss: weighted MSE with weight |clip(y)| is +0.017 over plain MSE. Its optimum is the maximiser of the weighted Pearson.
  • Recurrent core: a nonlinear gated unit. Every linear recurrence lost.
  • Depth: two layers (+0.003 over one). Beyond two, only residual paths with a bounded normalisation train, and they do not fit the time limit.
  • Pretraining labels: a 100 % teacher average, six causal teachers plus one future-window convolution teacher at six times their weight. The raw target only adds noise the student cannot explain.
  • Fine-tuning targets: 0.2 times the true value plus 0.8 times a cross-fitted look-ahead label. The one clear negative was a 100-row teacher’s label (−0.0003).
  • Fine-tuning procedure: all parameters, batch 32, lr 2e-4, 6 epochs, unscored-row weight 0.1. The scored rows are about three quarters of mask fine-tuning’s +0.007, new market data the rest.
  • Ensemble: members of different structures. All members share 99 % of their errors.
  • Inference: the kernels are bound by weight bandwidth (4.3 MB of float32 weights per row, more than a core’s L2 cache), so halving the bytes with IEEE half weights was the one speed lever that worked. Arithmetic tricks that win with a hot cache lose when the weights stream.

Neutral after fine-tuning: the head, the input front for a single model, memory length (the model uses about the last 500 rows, and wiping older memory costs nothing), capacity beyond 2×256, soups (+0.00014), replay (+0.00003). One block leaks: any normalisation across time inside a chunk lets row t see later rows (a fake 0.741).

7.5 Why nothing inside the network helps

  • A model run backwards over the sequence scores 0.99. The target is a function of the future price path, and a causal model can only learn its expectation given the past.
  • The bulk of the error is runs of same-direction errors. A stretch of hundreds of rows waits for the same price move; if the model guesses it wrong, the whole stretch is wrong. Correcting each sequence’s level with the true targets would be worth +0.029, level and scale +0.042. The error of a 50-row stretch correlates 0.72 with the previous stretch’s.
  • Nothing visible in advance predicts those runs: i1’s moves, the clock columns and i0’s own moves all correlate at 0.03 or less with the next stretch’s error. Online correction with look-ahead teachers as delayed labels gains exactly 0 (the fitted correction strength is 0 every time). A +0.060 reported along the way turned out to be an oracle that peeked at the future and was retracted.
  • The architectures’ advantages are real but solve problems this task does not have: very long context, parallel training, exact recall, irregular sampling, spatial structure between columns. What the task rewards is short nonlinear state-switching memory, clean labels and specialisation to the scored rows. The few architecture changes that helped all fit that: a second nonlinear stage, and depth made trainable by residual + DyT.

7.7 Lessons about method

  1. A smoke signal is a reason to run the full recipe, never a conclusion.
  2. A hold-out drawn from the set the fine-tune fits rewards fitting it. Cross-fit over all sequences instead.
  3. Divide a gate gain by three to get the expected public gain for a label change; expect nothing for data from another regime.
  4. Labels made from a model’s own out-of-fold prediction inflate the gate. Run the nested check before trusting them.
  5. Gate gains under +0.0004 do not show on the board. Do not spend a slot on them.
  6. One change per public slot, or the result cannot be attributed.
  7. The cost: 71 pod queues, 47 local queues and 633 run directories bought +0.0028 on the board between 10-17 and 10-20.
